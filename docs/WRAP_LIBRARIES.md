# Text wrapping: existing libraries compared (isolated test, 2026-10-14; nothing in the app changed or installed)
Test: scratchpad `lib_test/` (npm install with `--ignore-scripts` in an empty folder; served on localhost:8766; the page reuses the `wrap_poc` GPS card, the app's Inter font and a copy of `roughifySvgFragment` with the same options). Cases: A = the stored GPS card (title 280 px in a 240 px card), B = the same card with deliberately long labels. Usable width 216 px (12 px padding). Measured on screen with `getBBox`.

## Test results (screen measurements after wrapping, same card and font)
- Case A: pretext 2 title lines, widest 205 px, inside the card; svg-text same; d3-textwrap same width but title 56 px off centre; inline-size nothing changed (280 px wide, 20 px past each side).
- Case B: pretext and svg-text 8 lines, widest 216 px, inside the sides, 3 px (pretext) and 6 px (svg-text) past the card bottom; d3-textwrap 11 px past the bottom and off centre. All three overlap the next text block (3 pairs) because the lines below are not moved.
- Pretext detail found: its line `text` keeps a trailing space; its `width` is right (215.7 px, equal to the trimmed SVG width). Untrimmed, the tspan renders 6 px wider; `trimEnd()` fixes it.
- With about 15 lines of our own glue fed by pretext (first baseline stays, each following text moves down by the extra lines, card height = last baseline + padding): A card 132 px tall, 0 overlaps, 0 px past any side; B card 215 px tall, 0 overlaps, 0 px past any side, text centred.

## Best existing solution
`@chenglou/pretext`: the only one that measures with the real font, returns heights and line counts without touching the DOM, keeps `text-anchor` working (we build the tspans), and is actively released (10 versions since March 2026, last 2026-09-07). Limits: version 0.0.x (API may still change), it only lays out text (we create the tspans, line height 1.2 em is our choice), the font string must match the font actually used (a mismatch changes widths; the page's CSS Inter, not the stored Arial), and the font must be loaded before measuring.
Fallback if a dependency is unwanted: `@josephuspaye/svg-text` (2 KB, one 2020 release, almost no use) gives the same lines by measuring real text, but is unmaintained.

## What still needs our own code
1. Building the tspans and keeping them centred/aligned like the original text.
2. Finding the card around each text (rect, circle or ellipse) and its usable width.
3. Moving the text that follows and growing the card (the layout decision; it changes what the model drew and is for the owner).
4. Circle and ellipse containers (inscribed width), other shapes sitting under the card, and the validator/bounds side.
5. Where to run it: in the browser only (needs the real font); the stored SVG keeps the model's long lines unless the wrapped result is saved back.

## Can text be tied to its container? (stored data, read-only, 2026-10-14)
All 162 `<text>` elements in the 41 stored draw calls. Rule tested: the text's anchor point (x for centred text, x+2 for left-aligned, y minus 0.3 em) lies inside a rect, or inside a circle or ellipse by its equation, within the SAME draw call.
- One clear candidate: 58. Nested (frame plus inner shape; the innermost is the obvious owner): 48. Overlapping but not nested: 3 ("Internal Microphone" on headphones p.2 and p.3, "Receives Signals" on GPS p.1: a circle crossing a frame edge). No candidate: 53.
- Of the 53 with none: 21 are in calls with no shape at all (titles, paragraphs, lists: correctly unowned); 16 are within 40 px of a shape in the same call; 16 are farther.
- Doubtful even with one candidate: 9 of the 106 have under 60 percent of their estimated text box inside the chosen shape (a long line that overflows its card or ellipse).
- Cross-call: 0 texts lie inside a shape drawn by a different call, so one call is a complete unit for this.
- Failure: left-aligned card lines whose anchor sits just OUTSIDE the card because the card is too short: `inheritance_detail` (oops, p.1) `return "Animal sound"` 1 px below its card and `class Dog(Animal):` 21 px below; `external_microphone_detail` (headphones p.3) the last line 16 px below; `real_world_example_diagram` (p.5) 15 px below. Anchor containment says "no candidate" for text that belongs to the card. Captions beside a shape ("Possible places", "Distance", "All spheres overlap here", 10 to 30 px away) look identical to those, so a distance rule cannot tell an overflowing card line from a caption.
- Ambiguity rules that held: innermost wins for nested shapes; for the 3 overlapping cases the smaller shape (the circle) is the right owner.
Verdict: about two thirds (106 of 162) have a usable owner with an innermost-wins rule; the rest are unowned titles/paragraphs (21), captions and overflowing card lines that need a second rule (32), or doubtful (9). Reliable enough to extend the prototype to labels inside shapes; not to captions or text that starts below its card.

## Wrapping tested on the real cards (isolated, 2026-10-14; scratchpad `lib_test/cards.html`, pretext + browser measurement + the app's Rough copy)
Units: the 41 stored drawings as they stand on the 15 pages (the one edited drawing in its current form). Rule: a text belongs to the innermost rect that contains its anchor; texts in circles/ellipses, overlapping shapes, or outside every shape are left alone. Wrap at 12 px padding with pretext; move the later rows of the same rect down by the extra lines; grow the rect by that much (keeping its own bottom clearance); nothing else is moved.
- Tested: 25 rects with owned text: 13 cards (nothing inside them) and 12 containers (frames holding circles, bars, cards). 2 cards needed no change (abstraction, noise_cancellation_process). 11 cards were changed.
- Text itself: in all 11 changed cards the owned text ends inside the sides and bottom, with no overlap between the card's own lines (before: up to 219 px past the card).
- Succeeded cleanly (5 of 11): gps_signal_content, gps_phone_calculates_distance, python_oops_intro, encapsulation, inheritance.
- Problems from the neighbours (6 of 11): gps_satellite_intro (the card is a chip on an ellipse; grown 33 px it covers the satellite's label); polymorphism (grown 20 px it ends 10 px below its frame); inheritance_detail (2 text lines that sit below the card are left behind and now overlap it); external_microphone_detail (grown 101 px it overlaps a circle and a text; 1 line left behind); microphones_explained (grown 39 px into the next drawing `sound_wave_basics`); external_mic_intro (grown 75 px it passes the bottom of the canvas: 815 > 800).
- Containers: 12 tested, 2 changed (GPS p7, p8 ground bars, the text sat on the bottom edge; grew 18 and 13 px); no side/bottom overflow left; 7 left-behind captions near them.
- Arrows: none of the 11 changed cards had an arrow or connector in the grown area (no path/line hit); the hits are texts, circles, frames and the canvas edge.
- Uncertain: texts below a card whose anchor is outside it (4 cards) can't be owned; a card that is really a chip over another shape; growing 17 px to fix a 4 px overflow (the padding rule is generous); the vertical room below the card is not known to the browser-side code unless it reads the whole page.
Reading: wrapping and moving the card's own text is reliable; resizing a card is safe about half the time. The rest needs a rule for room (stop at the frame/neighbour/canvas, then shrink the font a step or ask the tutor to redraw), which is a design decision, not a measurement.

## Preflight check for growing a card (detection only, 2026-10-14; scratchpad `lib_test/preflight.html`)
Nothing is drawn or moved. For each of the 11 changed cards: extra height from pretext, the added strip below the card (card width x extra height), and every other element on the whole page (all draw calls, bounding boxes measured in the original page, the card's own rect and owned texts excluded). Rules: BLOCKED when the strip overlaps another element by more than 8 px2 that did not already overlap the card, when the card would end below the canvas (800) or below the frame that encloses it; elements that already overlapped the card (intentional overlap) are listed separately and, if the strip grows that overlap, the card is UNCERTAIN unless something else blocks it; otherwise SAFE.
- Result: 11 of 11 classified as observed in the earlier render test (5 SAFE = the 5 clean cases; 6 BLOCKED = the 6 problem cases); 0 wrong, 0 uncertain.
- Reasons: gps_satellite_intro blocked by the satellite's own label text (2482 px2); polymorphism by its frame (card would end 10 px below it); inheritance_detail by a left-behind code line (696 px2); external_microphone_detail by a text (4909 px2) and a circle (400 px2); microphones_explained by `sound_wave_basics` text in another draw call (10873 px2); external_mic_intro by the canvas (card would end at y=815). Existing overlap noted, not blocking: microphones_explained already overlaps `overview_components`.
- Extra heights: +17 to +101 px; the strips are 3,300 to 34,500 px2.
Caveats: all 11 are the same cards the rules were designed on (the frame exemption and the existing-overlap rule were added after the first run), so this is in-sample; no card outside these 11 was predicted. Rough.js strokes and text bounding boxes are approximate (browser `getBBox`); a 12 px padding or a different overlap threshold could move a borderline case.

## Validation of the preflight on new cases (isolated, 2026-10-14; scratchpad `lib_test/validate.html` + `validate_main.js`; detector rules unchanged)
The detector code is the one from `preflight.html`, copied without changes. Independent check: the grown card (rect and wrapped text, drawn with the Rough copy) and every other element of the page are rasterised as two layers (strokes and text only, fills off) and the pixels where both have ink are counted; the baseline is the card as originally drawn (before the longer text). BLOCKED = 40 or more new ink-overlap pixels or the card leaving the canvas; SAFE = 8 or fewer; between = borderline. I also looked at the renders of the disagreements.
Control: on the 11 development cards the ink check reproduces the earlier labels (11 of 11), so the method agrees with the render test the rules were tuned on. (A first run used the long-text drawing as baseline and mislabelled 3 cases; fixed before the results below.)
New cases: 29 (the 11 development cards are not counted)
- 4 real untouched cards with longer text (abstraction x1 and x2, noise_cancellation_process x1 and x3): 4 correct.
- 5 development cards with 2x text (other growth, same place): 5 correct.
- 20 synthetic placements on the real GPS p.2 page: 17 correct, 2 incorrect, 1 uncertain.
Total new: 26 correct, 2 incorrect, 1 uncertain (the correct ones include 11 BLOCKED and 15 SAFE).
Incorrect (both SAFE by the detector, BLOCKED by the ink check and by looking at the render):
- a vertical arrow leaving the card's bottom (the grown card now covers its first 36 px);
- a horizontal line under the card (the grown card cuts through it).
Cause (verified): a straight `line` or `path` has a zero-width or zero-height bounding box (`getBBox` gives w=0 or h=0), so its intersection area with the added strip is 0 and never exceeds the 8 px2 threshold. Connectors and underlines are therefore invisible to the detector. A horizontal arrow beside the card was correctly SAFE.
Uncertain: a card sitting as a chip on a big ellipse; the detector says UNCERTAIN (it grows an existing overlap), the ink check says BLOCKED; visually the grown card hides most of the ellipse, which could be intended; left as a human call.
Correct in the harder environments: caption 8 px below (blocked) / beside / 150 px below (safe); canvas bottom without and with room; inside a frame with and without room; two nested-frame cases (inner too short: blocked; outer too short but inner fine: safe); another draw call 4 px below (blocked) and 150 px below (safe); a neighbour card 10 px to the right (safe, growth is downward); a circle in the same draw call (blocked); text 30 px below (blocked). One of my nested-frame scenarios ("inner too small", inner frame bottom 610) was mis-specified: the card ends at 596, so SAFE is right and it does not test a frame exit; the two added nested cases do.
Caveats: 29 new cases, 20 of them hand-made on one real page, so they cover the scenario types I thought of; real sessions can differ. The ink check shares the Rough copy and text measurement with the detector, and its thresholds (40 and 8 pixels) are mine. The detector's rules were not changed, so the thin-line miss is still open.

## Thin lines and arrow heads in the preflight (isolated experiment, 2026-10-14; scratchpad `lib_test/thin_main.js` + `thin.html`; the existing detector is V0, unchanged)
Setup: the validation card (60,500,300x70, grows 36 px, so the added area is x 60..360, y 570..606) on the real GPS p.2 page, plus one other element in a second group, 13 cases. Truth is the rendered ink of the grown card against the ink of everything else (strokes, text, arrow-head fills), thresholds as before (40 px or more = blocked, 8 or fewer = safe, between = borderline), plus a look at the render. The board's own arrow marker (WhiteboardCanvas.tsx: viewBox 0 0 10 10, ref 8,5, 8x8 in stroke-width units, orient auto-start-reverse) was used.
Variants: V1 raw box widened by half the stroke width; V2 exact test of the stroked segment against the area for straight lines (line, 2-point polyline, path `M x y L x y`), V1 for anything else; V3 and V4 add the arrow head (marker box from the marker's own geometry, scaled by the stroke width, turned to the line direction) to V1 and V2; V5 (extra, not requested) walks any path with `getPointAtLength` every 2 px and adds the arrow head.
Results on the 11 cases with a clear truth (5 blocked: vertical arrow from the card bottom, horizontal line under it, diagonal across the corner, line starting 20 px inside, curve across; 6 safe: lines 4 px and 24 px below the area, a line beside the card, a diagonal and a curve whose box overlaps the area but whose line does not, an arrow far below):
- V0 existing: 6 correct, 3 missed (both known failures and the line starting inside), 2 false positives (the diagonal and the curve, whose boxes have area).
- V1 stroke-widened box: 9 correct, 0 missed, 2 false positives (diagonal, curve). Fixes both known failures.
- V2 segment test: 10 correct, 0 missed, 1 false positive (the curve; the diagonal is now right).
- V3 and V4 (with heads): same as V1 and V2; they also flag the arrow whose line is 4.5 px outside but whose head reaches in.
- V5 walk the path: 11 of 11 (0 missed, 0 false positives, curves right).
Borderline truth (not counted): the arrow head touching the card's bottom-right corner (ink 37; V3/V4/V5 flag it, V1/V2 do not; the render shows the tip touching the corner, a small intrusion) and a line that only touches the area's edge (ink 12; every variant says SAFE).
No variant blocked any of the safe nearby lines (4 px away, 24 px away, 10 px beside, touching only).
Why V0 failed: a straight horizontal or vertical `line`/`path` has a zero-height or zero-width box, so the overlap area is 0. Diagonals and curves have area, so V0 already blocked them (with false positives).
The stored drawings: 24 `path` elements (14 straight `M..L`, 10 curves or arcs), 0 `line`, `polyline`, or `polygon`; 15 `path` uses carry an arrow marker. So the model draws every connector as a `path`, straight in 58 percent and curved in 42 percent: the segment test (V2) alone would still treat the curved ones by box.
Complexity: V1 about 3 lines; V2 about 15 (path parse, clipping); arrow heads about 25 (marker size, viewBox scale, reference point, direction at the end point); V5 about 10 plus the arrow heads, and walking a path of 1,000 px costs 500 samples.
Browser compatibility: `getBBox`, `getTotalLength`, `getPointAtLength` and `getComputedStyle` work in all current browsers; the experiment ran in one Chromium only. Not handled: other marker settings (`preserveAspectRatio`, numeric `orient`, `marker-mid`), round or square line caps that extend past the ends, dashes, filters, and `transform` (the sanitiser removes it).
Not done (as asked): the detector was not changed, nothing was merged, no drawing was modified.

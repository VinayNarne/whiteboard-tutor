# Shape model and shadow validator: findings (phase 1 and the live log-only hook, 2026-10-14)

Scope: a pure shape model and a shadow comparison, as approved. **Nothing in the application changed**: `engine/shape_model.py` is a new module that no production code imports; `tests/shadow_validator.py` is an offline runner; `tests/test_shape_model.py` holds 31 unit and property tests. The existing validator stays authoritative; no rule was weakened. Design: `docs/VALIDATOR_DESIGN.md`. Raw run data: `docs/shadow_runs/`.
Tags: **DEMONSTRATED** (measured or run in this phase), **IN-SAMPLE** (measured, but the rule or threshold was tuned on the same drawings, so it does not yet show generalisation), **ASSUMPTION** (not tested).

## 1. What was built
- `extract_shapes`: SVG to a flat list of shapes in paint order (rect, circle/ellipse, polygon, connector, text label; style inherited from parent groups; text with `tspan` lines split per line; paths and arcs flattened to about 4 px pieces; arrow heads of the board's three markers as small polygons).
- Roles from geometry only: label, connector, node, container (holds two or more other regions, or one of at least 15 percent of its area), decoration (thinner than 20 px).
- `find_collisions`: the rules over every pair of shapes; **a shape's call, element and id are never used by a rule**, only geometry, style and paint order. `shadow_check(existing, new)` charges a call only with collisions that involve its own shapes. Every rule is a pure function with a documented threshold (all thresholds are assumptions until tuned).
- Shadow runner: replays drawings through the existing checks (`validate_geometry`, `check_overlap`) and the proposal, call by call, and records both verdicts with reasons and the time taken.
- Not done (needs approval): the one-line log-only call inside `_handle_draw_new_element` that would record the proposal's verdict on live drawings. Until then "shadow mode" means offline replay.

## 2. Partial overlaps inside one call (design decision 3)
Instead of making all overlaps inside a call legal, the rules look at what an overlap does, the same in one call or many:
- Two shapes where the one on top lets the lower one show through (no fill, or a group with opacity 0.6 or less): legal. Opaque on opaque with more than 20 percent of the smaller covered: rejected; stacked duplicates (90 percent covered, similar size): rejected; a shape fully inside a larger one (at most 80 percent of its area): nested, legal; a marker of at most 15 percent of the area of a node it overlaps (a dot on a circle's edge): legal; the same marker on a frame's edge: rejected.
- DEMONSTRATED (unit tests): outline Venn legal, the same circles opaque rejected, outline over fill legal, a group at 0.4 opacity legal.
- DEMONSTRATED (stored GPS drawings with intersecting spheres, `shadow_validator.py venn`): seven drawings with 3 to 10 regions each, as ONE call and as ONE CALL PER SHAPE. The existing validator accepts every one as a single call but rejects 4, 6, 8, 10, 7, 10 and 7 of their per-shape calls (the same shapes, in the order p4 `gps_circle_demo`, `gps_two_circles`, `gps_three_circles`, p5, p6, p7, p8). The proposal accepts the five drawings with intersecting spheres or rings in both forms (GPS p4 two and three circles, p5, p6, p7), rejects the same collisions in both forms for the other two (p4 `gps_circle_demo`, p8), and the final collisions are identical in all seven (property: collisions of the final page do not depend on grouping).
- **Rendering fact found on the way (DEMONSTRATED, rendered):** the board's Rough.js conversion (`roughRenderer.ts`) copies only stroke, stroke width and fill colour of a shape. `fill-opacity` and `opacity` on a shape never reach the screen (checked: the output paths have no opacity). So a "translucent" Venn written with `fill-opacity` is drawn opaque on the default hand-drawn board; only `fill="none"` or an `opacity` on a parent group survives. The model treats shape-level opacity as ineffective (`RENDERER_DROPS_SHAPE_OPACITY`). Fixing the renderer is outside this phase.

## 3. Results
**Connector regression cases (the six-box diagram, DEMONSTRATED).** The proposal's collisions on the final page are identical in the four groupings (A one call, B boxes then connectors, C one call per connector, D one call per box), as drawn: 2 opaque overlaps (the label pills over the User box), 7 connectors through a box, 8 through label text, 4 + 3 arrow heads in a box or on a label. The existing validator: A accepts everything, B/C/D reject one call each (the pills call or the connector call, by union box). With the same boxes and edge-attached connectors (9 connectors in the gaps, labels in free space, stroke 2): the proposal accepts every call in every grouping (0 rejections, 0 advisories); the existing validator rejects 2 calls that are fine (B: the connectors call, C: the elbow connector), both because of the union box. The union-box problem (problem 1) is therefore fixed by the shape comparison, and the thin-connector and in-call problems (2 and 3) are covered by the same rules.
**Stored corpus, 44 calls (the old 41, the two test notebooks and the first live smoke run), IN-SAMPLE.** The existing validator accepts all 44 on replay. After the owner decision that label crossings stay advisory, the proposal would reject 17 (39 percent): collisions by count: label straddling a box edge 17, connector through a node 14, arrow head in a node 10, opaque overlap 6, label on label 4. Recorded as advisories only: label overflow 49, connector over a label 26, arrow head over a label 10, connectors on top of each other 5. Time per call: mean 16 ms, maximum 80 ms.
**Independent check of the text rules (DEMONSTRATED, rendered with Chromium and Inter).** For each of the 17 final pages of the original corpus I counted the pixels where text ink overlaps stroke ink, and where two texts overlap. A page "has a text collision" at 30 or more text-on-stroke pixels or 10 or more text-on-text pixels (my thresholds). Truth: 13 pages with a collision, 4 clean (headphones p6, the web-app boxes, GPS p5 and p6). Counting only what could ever be enforced (label on label, label straddling a box, a label hidden by a later box), the proposal finds the collision on 5 of the 13 pages and agrees with the truth on 9 of 17 pages; adding the connector-over-label advisories it flags 12 of the 13 and none of the 4 clean ones (16 of 17 pages agree; the miss is GPS p7, the title crossing the outline of the big ellipse, which is only a label-overflow advisory). Reading: most of the text collisions in the stored drawings are connectors running over labels, and under the owner's decision those are recorded, not enforced. Caveats: page-level only, text rules only, thresholds are mine, and the marker ratio and role rules were adjusted while looking at this same corpus.
**Known collision failures (screenshot review):** headphones p4 and p5 (frame drawn over an earlier paragraph; circle crossing the frame): caught (label straddling a container, label on label, opaque overlap). Headphones p3 (circle over the frame edge) caught; its card lines spilling out of the card are an advisory. Headphones p1 and p2 (labels wider than their circles, arrows over labels): arrows over labels caught; the width problem is an advisory. GPS p1 (chip over the satellite label, label over the phone, arrow ending inside the phone circle) caught; GPS p2 and p3 (line through "Satellite" and "Phone") caught; GPS p4 (labels overprinting) caught; GPS p8 caught; the oops cards (lines spilling below a card) caught; the Test 1 flowchart (labels on arrows, a pill over the connect box) caught; the Test 2 page (boxes only) accepted. Not caught: GPS p7 title over an outline. Several of these are now advisory-level (connector over a label).
**Text layout (DEMONSTRATED).** Width estimate: on 177 stored labels measured in Chromium, real width per character per point is 0.49 (median, regular), 0.52 (bold), 5th to 95th percentile 0.45 to 0.55; the existing 0.55 over-reports (20 of 177 labels are wider than it). The model uses 0.50 and 0.52. A label centred in a region that sticks out of it is an advisory (`label_overflow`, 48 on the final pages; the GPS card title is one), a label whose centre is outside a region it overlaps is a rejection. Unit tests: label in its own box legal; straddling the edge, label on label, label hidden by a later opaque box rejected.

## 4. What is demonstrated and what is assumed
DEMONSTRATED: the model runs on the whole corpus and the six-box variants; verdicts on the final page do not depend on grouping (4 groupings, 7 stored drawings, 20 random splits); the existing validator is inconsistent for the same shapes by grouping and the proposal is not; the union-box rejections vanish; thin connectors and arrow heads are detected; the page-level text agreement; the width calibration; the opacity rendering fact; per call cost in the tens of milliseconds.
IN-SAMPLE: the marker ratio (0.15; the stored dots are 2 to 12 percent of the circle they sit on, chips and pills 20 percent and more), the container rule, the label-overflow classification, the page agreement.
ASSUMPTIONS (untested): that thresholds hold on new sessions; that the false-rejection rate on good drawings stays acceptable (17 of 43 stored calls are rejected, and the screenshot review and the pixel check say most of them really are collisions, but not all 17 were examined one by one); that the model will react to the new messages by attaching connectors at edges (the earlier overlap-message round did not reduce rejections); that role guessing (node or container) is reliable on drawings unlike the stored ones; the performance of a 25-element page; hand-drawn wobble of 2 to 3 px against the 4 px band; behaviour with text on curves, rotated text (not allowed), and the `modify` path (not covered by this phase).

## 5. Known weaknesses of the proposal, found while building it
- Labels written ON an arrow (edge labels) are reported as connectors through labels; the 2 px and 6 px margins and the width estimate (about plus or minus 10 percent) decide borderline cases.
- A frame's role depends on what it holds; a frame with one box inside is a node, so a circle poking out of it can be a "marker". The container rule is a heuristic.
- A label whose centre is inside a big shape but which crosses its outline (the GPS p7 title) is only an advisory.
- Rule results depend on paint order (a box drawn after a label hides it), which is real rendering behaviour but means "a page" must be the stored order, not a set.
- Roles can change when content is added later (a box becomes a container once something is drawn in it), so the same connector can be legal or not depending on what was drawn first, as with the existing validator.

## 6. Live log-only hook (owner-approved conditions, built and verified)
What it is: `engine/shadow.py`, called once from `_handle_draw_new_element` right after the sanitiser (four lines in `engine/author.py`, three new event names in `core/events.py`). For every drawing that reaches that point it logs one `shadow_validated` event: the existing verdict (the same `validate_geometry` and `check_overlap` the handler applies next, so the pair is recorded together), the proposed verdict, the collision categories with counts, up to six reasons (kind, bounding boxes, element ids), up to three advisories, shape counts, and the time. No label text and no SVG source are logged (a label appears as its length); a record is about 2 KB.
- **Authoritative:** the handler ignores the hook's result. The hook runs in a worker thread, errors are caught and logged as `shadow_failed`, a time limit of 0.5 s abandons it (`shadow_skipped`), and pages with more than 600 shapes are skipped.
- **Switch:** `LP_SHADOW_VALIDATOR=0` (also off, false, no) disables it completely; it is on by default while data are collected.
- **Advisory by decision:** label overflow, a connector or arrow head over a label, and connectors on top of each other are recorded as advisories and never count towards the proposed verdict. Thresholds stay provisional.
- **Not touched:** opacity rendering, prompts, tool schemas, the existing validation rules.
- **Reading the data:** `python scripts/shadow_report.py [--since 2026-10-15] [--json out.json]` prints record counts, sessions, the existing-versus-proposed matrix, categories, the drawings where the two differ, and the overhead.
Verification done before commit: the full in-process suite 723 passed, 32 skipped (712 before, plus 11 new hook tests); `tests/test_shadow_hook.py` runs the real draw handler on 15 scenarios (the six-box variants as drawn and edge-attached in four groupings, a plain card, an overlap rejection, a canvas bleed, a transform, labels overlapping inside one call, a background frame, malformed XML) with the hook on and off: outcomes, stored content and browser events are identical; the same for a failing hook, a slow hook (the draw is delayed less than the limit) and a whole chat turn through the API with a scripted model. `tests/shadow_overhead.py` replays the 109 stored and variant drawings four rounds with the hook on and off through the real handler (audio faked): outcomes identical; the hook adds a mean of 16 ms per drawing (median 7 ms, 95th percentile 78 ms, maximum 139 ms; compute maximum 170 ms), against 8 to 15 s of model time and about 2 s of speech per drawing in a real turn.
First live record (one real turn of the architecture prompt on the running backend): the model drew all six boxes and the connectors in one element, the existing validator accepted it, the proposal would have rejected it (5 connectors through boxes, 3 arrow heads in boxes), 4 label crossings and 4 doubled connectors recorded as advisories, 10 ms overhead. One record is not evidence; it only shows the live path writes what it should.

## 7. Remaining limitations (unchanged by the hook)
- All thresholds are provisional and were tuned on the stored drawings. The proposal's reject-level text rules find only 5 of 13 pages that the pixel check calls colliding; the rest are connector-over-label advisories.
- Text width is an estimate (about plus or minus 10 percent); roles (node or container) are inferred from geometry; paint order matters; the rendering ignores shape-level opacity, so see-through overlap is only `fill="none"` or a group opacity.
- `modify_existing_element` is not recorded (the hook is on draw only), and the shadow record uses the page as stored, so it shows what the proposal would say, not what the model would do after a rejection (the earlier overlap-message round did not reduce rejections).
- A drawing rejected before the sanitiser (transform, background frame, duplicate id) has no shadow record.
- The data come from my tests and your sessions so far; they are not yet a representative sample.

## 8. Review plan (before any enforcement is proposed)
**Trigger (owner-approved):** at least 150 recorded drawings, from at least 15 sessions, on 5 or more different topics, over at least 2 days. `python scripts/shadow_report.py` prints the trigger status on every run (have / need for each number) and says MET or not met yet. Sessions from automated test runs can be left out with `--exclude`. Status when this was written: 1 drawing, 1 session, 1 topic, 1 day (the smoke run), so not met; no review is due and none will be started before the trigger.
**What the review will contain** (this is a decision point, not an automatic approval for enforcement):
1. Agreement and disagreement counts, each collision category's rate per drawing, the overhead percentiles.
2. **Coverage.** Records carry the shape roles of each drawing, so the report splits the data by type (connector-heavy: 4 or more connectors; text-heavy: 8 or more labels; simple: up to 5 shapes; mixed) and by size (1 to 5, 6 to 15, 16 or more shapes), with the proposed verdict per type, and by topic and day. If connector-heavy or text-heavy drawings are thin in the data, the review says so and does not draw conclusions for them. (Records written before the roles field was added are counted as "unknown" type.)
3. **Visual inspection.** `python scripts/shadow_review.py` writes `logs/shadow_review.html` (not committed): up to 20 disagreements (taken across the categories, so no single kind dominates) and 10 agreements (half both-accept, half both-reject), each drawn as the model wrote it, with the page contents at that moment in grey and the blamed shapes boxed (red dashed = a rejection reason, orange = advisory). Drawings the existing validator rejected are included, because the sheet reads them from the log. I will look at each card, record whether the proposal was right, and report false positives and false negatives by looking, not only by counts.
4. **Missing coverage, stated with numbers** (the report's "known gaps" line): drawings rejected before the sanitiser (transform, background frame, duplicate id) have no shadow record; `modify_existing_element` calls are not recorded at all (the report counts them next to the drawing calls); a record shows what the proposal says about the page as it was, not what the model would do after a rejection; records from automated runs versus real sessions are not distinguished unless excluded; text width, roles and thresholds remain provisional.
5. Open decisions then: whether any enforcement is proposed and in what order (the shape-level cross-element check first, since it only removes false rejections), the rejection message wording, whether any advisory should ever be enforced, whether to ask for the renderer to honour shape opacity, and connector references as a later option.
Until the review no further validator improvement is started, unless a critical issue is found.

## 9. Connector-versus-text diagnostic (log-only, added 2026-10-20)
Why: the connector-versus-text investigation (`docs/benchmark_runs/2026-10-15-controlled/connector_text_qa/`, real Chromium on the 33 saved boards) found 25 visible issues on 13 boards and 41 pairs where a line merely passes under its own opaque label box. The older advisories (`connector_through_label`, `arrowhead_on_label`) are geometry only: on the same 33 pages they report 57 and 5 pairs. They stay as they are.
What: `engine/connector_text.py` (pure) is called by `shadow.compute` and its result is written into the same `shadow_validated` record as `connector_text`: counts of `body` (a line over visible text), `head` (an arrow head over visible text), `head_covered` (a later opaque box that holds a label hides the head), `ambiguous`, `hidden`, up to four items (kind, certainty, a fixed reason code, the amount, the two shapes' bounding boxes and element ids; a label appears as its length) and `unsupported` counts. No label text, no SVG.
Rules: the board paints in document order, so a sample of a connector that lies under a later opaque, filled shape is not visible and is only counted as `hidden` (shape-level opacity is dropped by the renderer, so it does not make a box see-through; a translucent parent group does). The arrow head is the real marker outline (the board's three markers, or the marker the drawing defines, scaled by the stroke width) and is judged apart from the line. Ink band: glyph height (0.78 em above and 0.10 em below the baseline), width from the 0.50 estimate. A definite finding needs 3 px of visible line or 6 px2 of visible head inside the band at 0.9 times the estimated width; the same overlap that exists only at 1.1 times, or a 1 to 3 px graze, is `ambiguous` and kept apart. Unsupported geometry (marker not found, a head wider than 300 px, a connector longer than 4000 px, a work budget of 600,000 points per page) is counted, never guessed.
Isolation: diagnostic only; it is never part of a verdict (`proposed`, `differs`, `reject_kinds` are unchanged), its exceptions are caught inside `compute` and recorded as `connector_text_error` while the rest of the record is still written, it runs under the same switch, time limit and 600-shape cap as the rest of the hook, and `shape_model.find_collisions`, the existing advisories and `engine/validator.py` are untouched (two additive `Shape` fields, `anchor` and `marker_ends`, were added for it).
Replay of the 33 saved boards (`python tests/connector_text_replay.py`, no model): 24 definite findings on 9 boards (14 line, 9 head, 1 head covered by a label box) and 12 ambiguous ones (4 boards have only ambiguous ones); 40 of the 41 known hidden-under-chip pairs suppressed, 1 reported as ambiguous (text-width estimate), 0 as definite; of 30 label rows the browser measurement lists as visible, 22 are definite and 8 ambiguous, none missed; 0 definite findings outside the browser list on the 33 boards. IN-SAMPLE: the thresholds were set looking at these boards and the check agrees with the measurement it was built from, so this is not evidence of generalisation. Cost: 4 ms mean (26 ms maximum) for the check on a final page; the whole record 34 ms mean, 324 ms maximum, as the hook already allows.
Known limits: text width is an estimate (the ambiguous class exists for that); `modify_existing_element` is not recorded, so KQQT's 2 definite findings (made by a modify) appear on the final page but in no call record; call-by-call, a finding charged to one call could later be hidden by a box drawn in a later call (0 cases on the 33 boards); drawing-order across calls is the stored order; filled arrow polygons and hand-built triangle heads are shapes, not heads, so they are not measured; tutor annotation arrows (the SDDL case) are drawn by the app from element bounds and are not in the stored geometry; severity (strong or minor) is not modelled, a 28 px head touching a caption counts as definite.
